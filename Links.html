CS 101 Lecture Engagement and Continuous Evaluation System

[Lecture portal] [Practice and labs] [Course page]

A browser-based system I designed and built for CS 101, which keeps in-person lectures and labs engaging for a class of about 150. Students log in from a phone or laptop with their institute Google account and solve short questions (MCQ, ordering, debug-it and programming) as each concept is taught. Answers are checked instantly in the browser, and a live dashboard shows how many students have understood each concept before the class moves on. Attendance and participation are recorded automatically, past lectures stay open for practice anytime, and the same system runs labs and exams with automatic grading. The practice question sets and labs are open to everyone.

Lab: Principles of Reliable Data Transfer

Courses I Recommend to Undergraduates

  • Software tools: The Missing Semester of Your CS Education (MIT). Shell, Git, editors, debugging and profiling.

  • Computer systems: Computation Structures (MIT 6.004). From transistors and gates to processors, memory hierarchies and operating systems.

  • Networking: CS 168: Introduction to the Internet (UC Berkeley), the course built by Scott Shenker and colleagues. Clear explanations of why the Internet is designed the way it is.

  • Machine learning basics: Neural Networks and Deep Learning by Michael Nielsen. A free online book that builds neural networks from first principles.

  • Probability: Probabilistic Systems Analysis and Applied Probability (MIT 6.041, John Tsitsiklis)

  • Linear algebra: Linear Algebra (MIT 18.06, Gilbert Strang)

  • Physics: The Theoretical Minimum by Leonard Susskind. Lecture courses in modern physics for anyone who knows a little math.
